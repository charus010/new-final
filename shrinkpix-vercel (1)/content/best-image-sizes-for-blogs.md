Uploading whatever your camera produces is the quickest way to slow a blog down. Right-sized images look sharp, load fast and keep your layout tidy. This guide gives practical dimensions and file size targets for the images most blogs use, along with the reasoning so you can adapt the numbers to your own theme.

## Dimensions versus file size

These are two separate things. Dimensions are the width and height in pixels, while file size is how many kilobytes the file occupies. A large image can be compressed to a small file, but the browser still has to decode every pixel. The best result comes from setting sensible dimensions first and then compressing.

## Find your layout width

Open your blog and measure the content column on a desktop screen. If articles are 760 pixels wide, an inline photo never needs to be wider than that on a standard display. For sharp results on high-density screens, upload at roughly one and a half to two times the displayed width. A 760 pixel column is therefore well served by images 1200 to 1500 pixels wide. Anything beyond that is wasted data.

## Featured and hero images

The large image at the top of a post is the most important one. A width of 1600 pixels is plenty for full-width layouts, with a typical height around 900 pixels for a sixteen by nine ratio. Aim for under 200 KB. Because this image is often the largest element on the page, its weight has a direct effect on how fast the page appears to load, so spend extra effort compressing it well.

## Inline article images

For photos inside the text, 1000 to 1400 pixels wide works for most themes. Keep them under 150 KB each. Screenshots with text may need to be wider to stay readable, but you can often crop away empty margins and save as an optimised PNG. If a screenshot is mostly flat colour, PNG will usually be smaller than JPG and look cleaner.

## Thumbnails and archive cards

Blog listings show small previews. Check what size your theme generates, commonly between 300 and 600 pixels wide, and upload a version close to that. Thumbnails should typically be under 50 KB. Many platforms create resized copies automatically, but they start from your upload, so a smaller source still helps both storage and speed.

## Social sharing images

When someone shares your post, platforms display a preview image. A size of 1200 by 630 pixels, a ratio of about 1.91 to 1, is widely recommended and works for Facebook, LinkedIn and most messaging apps. Keep important text and subjects away from the edges, because some apps crop. Specify the image with an Open Graph tag and keep the file below 300 KB so previews load quickly.

## Logos, icons and favicons

Logos are best as SVG when available, because they scale without loss and are tiny. If you must use a raster file, export a PNG at twice the display size with transparency. Favicons need a square image, typically 512 by 512 pixels, from which smaller sizes are generated. Keep these files as small as possible since they load on every page.

## File size targets at a glance

As a general guide: hero images under 200 KB, inline photos under 150 KB, thumbnails under 50 KB, social images under 300 KB and icons under 20 KB. If a page contains many pictures, keep the total image weight below about one megabyte for the first screen of content. These are guidelines, not hard rules, but they keep most blogs comfortably fast on mobile.

## Choosing formats

Use JPG for photographs, PNG for graphics with transparency or sharp text and WebP where your platform supports it. Whatever the format, compress before you upload and preview the result to confirm the quality still looks good.

## Build a repeatable workflow

Decide your standard widths once and write them down. Resize every new image to the matching size, compress at around 75 percent quality, give it a descriptive file name and upload. Doing this consistently takes only a minute per image and prevents slow pages from creeping in as your archive grows.

## Conclusion

Good blog images are not about using the biggest possible file. They are about matching dimensions to your layout, trimming file size and choosing the right format. Follow the targets above and your posts will look crisp, load quickly and perform better in search results.

## Quick reference table in words

For a typical blog, plan on 1600 by 900 pixels for headers, 1200 pixels wide for inline photos, 400 by 300 pixels for thumbnails, 1200 by 630 pixels for social previews and 512 by 512 pixels for the site icon. Adjust these to your theme, but write your chosen numbers in a note so every contributor follows the same standard and your library stays consistent.
